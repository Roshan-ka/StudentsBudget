# StudentsBudget
Budget app for students new to a city: see where your money goes across all your accounts and cash, check whether your budget is realistic for your city, and log spending in seconds. Installable web app; data stays on your phone unless you save it to your account.

## What it is
StudentsBudget helps you through your first months in a new city. You set up your rent, your income, what you want to spend each month and your accounts once, for example a local bank, a bank back home and some cash. The app compares your plan with typical costs in Regensburg, the only city it knows so far.

After that you log spending in a few seconds, with a line like "döner 6". The home screen shows how much is left to spend this month, and under it how much is safe to spend this week. Charts show where the money went. On Sundays, a short check-in looks back at the week.

You don't need an account: everything is stored on your device. If you want, create one with an email and a password, save your data to it, and load it onto another phone (see **Account** below).

## Install on iPhone
Install the app first, then set up. A Safari tab and the Home Screen app keep their data apart, so anything you enter in Safari stays in Safari.

1. Open the app's web address in Safari.
2. Tap Share, then Add to Home Screen.
3. Open the app from the Home Screen and set it up there.

On desktop Chrome, use the install icon at the right end of the address bar.

## Using it offline
Open the app once while you are online after each update. From then on it opens with no connection, and you can keep logging spending.

Exchange rates need a connection to refresh. Offline, the app shows the last rates it saved, each with the date it is from (Settings, then Exchange rates).

## Using the app
**Setup.** It asks only the basics: rent, your accounts, a few income sources and fixed costs, one-off costs, optional guesses and an amount for six envelopes, with an optional everyday budget (what you want to spend in a month, apart from rent and your fixed costs) at the top of that step. Blank amounts are fine, and a plan saves even when it adds up to more than your income; Home then suggests a budget. With an everyday budget, each envelope shows a suggested amount under what students here spend, taken from what the budget leaves after the envelopes you have set, and "Use the suggested amounts" fills only the envelopes you haven't set. Guesses you skip are not saved. If you live off savings, enter what you use each month as Other income and switch off "Add it automatically", so it is not logged as income. The other income sources, fixed costs and envelopes are under Settings, then Edit plan.

**Accounts.** New entries, quick add, transfers and new fixed costs start on your first euro bank account, or on any euro account if you have no euro bank account. To use another account, tap "Make default" next to it in Settings, then Accounts. A quick-add line with a euro sign ("döner 6 €") goes to a euro account when your default is in another currency. An account that is overdrawn can start below zero: tick "Overdrawn (below zero)" under its opening balance.

**Fixed costs and income.** Rent, your income and other fixed costs can be added for you on their day. Leave "Add it automatically" on in setup, or change it later under Settings, then Fixed costs and income, where you can also pause, resume and edit a line. A new or resumed line starts the next day, and setup and Settings say when its first entry comes. If the app was closed on that day, the entry is added the next time you open it. These entries are tagged "Recurring" in Entries. Don't log them by hand as well, or they count twice.

**Home.** The big number is what is left to spend this month across your envelopes: the whole monthly amounts minus what you have spent this month. The month you set up gets the whole amounts too, and its spending counts from the day you set up. Under it, "This week" is what was left of the month when the week began, split evenly over the weeks left in the month (Monday to Sunday, this one included, even if only one of its days is in the month), minus what you have spent so far this week. Going over reads as "€12 over". A suggested budget (an amount per category, from last month's spending or from typical Regensburg costs) shares out what is available, or your everyday budget when that is less, with each typical cost between the bottom and the middle of its range. It leads in the first week of the month, or when your envelopes add up to more than what is available. Otherwise it waits behind "See a suggested budget". "Use this budget" changes your envelopes only.

**Try this.** When an envelope has run over this month, or more than 90% of it is gone before the last 7 days, Home can show one line under it, such as "Try this: Eat at the Mensa instead of a restaurant · save about €10 per meal". Tap it to see how the swap works and where each price comes from, with the date it was checked. A saving that rests on an assumption, such as how often you travel, is marked "(estimate)" on the line and explained when you open it. The Sunday check-in shows the same line under a category that is over its share for the week. Nothing shows while an envelope is on track.

**Badges.** Four badges reward steady habits, never spending. They count only weeks and months in which you log spending on your envelopes yourself, by the date it is for. Fixed costs, entries added automatically, income and transfers don't count.
- **Under-Budget Week:** such a week in which you spend no more than its share of your envelopes: what was left of them at the start of the week, split over the weeks left in the month. Bronze at 4 weeks, silver at 12, gold at a whole semester: the weeks of the semester in your profile (at least 13), or 26 without one.
- **Logging Streak:** 4 weeks in a row in which you log envelope spending yourself.
- **Ramen Royalty:** a full week that costs less in groceries than every week before it.
- **Latte Survivor:** a month with at most half the eating out of your first full month that had some.

Each week (Monday to Sunday) is saved from the Wednesday after it ends, the next time you open the app, so expenses you log on Monday or Tuesday still count for it. After that it stays as it was, even if you change your plan later. Away for a week? Switch on "I was away this week" in the check-in, or "I was away last week" on Monday and Tuesday. Your streak keeps its count, and that week doesn't count towards Under-Budget Week or Ramen Royalty. Home shows how many badges you have and which one is next; "See all badges" opens all four with their full rules.

**Discounts.** The Tips tab has a second view, Discounts: student discounts in Regensburg, with no alcohol deals. Each shows the place, what you get, the conditions, the address and the page it comes from, with the date it was checked. Tried one? Tap "Still works" or "Doesn't work anymore". Your answer is saved on your phone, and tapping again changes it. "Last confirmed" is the later of the check date and your last "Still works". A discount last confirmed 8 or more weeks ago reads "May be outdated" and moves down the list; one you marked as not working goes to the end.

**Account.** Optional. Settings starts with **Your account** (when the app has an account server set up): create an account with an email and a password of 8 or more characters, or sign in. Signed in, **Save to my account** copies everything on the phone to your account. If your account holds something this phone doesn't (saved from another phone, or deleted here since), the app says how much would go and asks first. On a second phone, **Sign in to load it** at the top of setup leads to **Load from my account**, which replaces that phone's data with your account's once you confirm. Nothing updates by itself yet: save again after changes. Using two phones? Load on one before you change anything there, or its next save replaces what the other one saved. Signing out keeps the data on the phone. On iPhone, sign in inside the Home Screen app.

**Profile.** Settings has your profile right after your account, all optional: your name (Home then says "Hi" with it), your university (Universität Regensburg, OTH Regensburg or Other) and your semester dates. For the two Regensburg universities the dates fill in from the university's published semester dates, with the source shown, and you can still change them. Your semester sets gold for Under-Budget Week. With semester dates saved, the one-off costs step (in setup, or Settings, then Edit plan) offers the next semester fee in one tap. Where the Studierendenwerk has published that semester's fee, the amount is filled in with its sources; otherwise you fill it in. The fee is due in the month re-registration opens where your university has published it, else in the month before the semester starts.

**Charts.** Spending by category and income by source open as a donut. Switch each chart to Bars or Table. "Saved each month" and a category over time show Columns, Line or Table. One period selector (This month, 3, 6 or 12 months) applies to all of them, and the month switcher moves where the period ends. On This month, the two over-time charts show the last 6 months up to that month instead. A tap on a slice, bar or column opens those entries (except "Everything else"). For a 3, 6 or 12-month period, a slice or bar opens the entries of the period's end month, and a column opens its own month. A month with nothing logged says so, and still shows those two charts when the 6 months up to it have entries.

**Dates.** Full dates read DD-MM-YYYY. When you add or edit an entry, Today and Yesterday are one tap away.

## Privacy
- Your data stays in this browser's IndexedDB, on this device. Until you save to an account, there is no server copy.
- An account is optional (email and password). When you save to it, a copy of your accounts, entries, plan, guesses, manual exchange rates, weekly records, discount answers and profile is kept in a Supabase database in Frankfurt (EU). Row-level security lets each account read and change only its own rows. The copy is not encrypted end to end: whoever runs the Supabase project could technically read it.
- Your profile (name, university and semester dates, all optional), the weekly records behind your badges and your discount answers are stored on this device, go into any backup you export, and go into your account only when you save to it.
- No analytics and no third-party scripts. The fonts come from the app itself.
- Signed out, the only outgoing request is to `api.frankfurter.dev`, for exchange rates. It names the foreign currencies your accounts use. It carries none of your entries or amounts. Like any web request, it shows your IP address to that service. Signed in, the app also talks to the Supabase project: to sign in, to keep the sign-in fresh in the background, and when you save or load.
- A backup file is a plain JSON file that you keep. The app never uploads it. Anyone who has the file can read your data, so keep it somewhere you trust.
- Clearing the site's data in your browser, or removing the app, deletes your data. Export a backup regularly: Settings, then Backup, then Export backup. To get your data back, choose the file under **Restore from a backup** in Settings, or under **Have a backup? Restore it** at the bottom of the setup screen. The app names the file and says when it was saved and what it holds, then asks you to confirm **Replace all data**.

## Try it on your iPhone (HTTPS on your own Wi-Fi)
Offline use needs a service worker, and a service worker only runs on a secure address: HTTPS, or localhost. A plain-HTTP address such as `http://192.168.0.137:3100` is not secure, so the app cannot be tested offline on a phone that way. For a developer who wants to try the production build on a real iPhone, this serves it over HTTPS on your own Wi-Fi, with a certificate the phone trusts.

1. On the PC, install mkcert (`winget install FiloSottile.mkcert`) and run `mkcert -install` once.
2. Run `npm run cert:https`. It writes `certs/studentsbudget.pem` and `certs/studentsbudget-key.pem` for localhost, this PC's private IP addresses and its `.local` name, and prints the folder of the mkcert root certificate (`mkcert -CAROOT`). It never runs `mkcert -install` for you.
3. Get the root certificate onto the iPhone: `rootCA.pem` in the printed `mkcert -CAROOT` folder, by email to yourself or through iCloud/OneDrive. Open it, then Settings → General → VPN & Device Management → install the profile. Then Settings → General → About → Certificate Trust Settings → turn on full trust for mkcert.
4. Run `npm run start:https` and open the printed `https://…:3443` address in Safari. It builds first, so the offline copy matches the commit. `HTTPS_PORT`, `HTTPS_CERT` and `HTTPS_KEY` change the port (3443 by default) and the certificate files. When you run it, Windows may ask whether Node may use the network: allow it on the private network.
5. Tap Share, then Add to Home Screen. Open the app from the Home Screen while you are still online and set it up there (see Install on iPhone: the Home Screen app keeps its own data). Then try airplane mode.

**Keep the address stable.** The app's data belongs to the exact address (https, host and port). A new IP address or port means an empty app. Reserve the PC's IP address in your router, or use the `.local` name, and export a backup before you change the address. The certificate covers the addresses the PC had when you ran `npm run cert:https`. If its IP changes, run it again. The `.local` name works only if the phone can resolve it; try it once before relying on it.

**Safety.** The mkcert root lets this PC vouch for any site on that phone. Keep `rootCA-key.pem` private, and remove the profile from the phone when the testing is over (Settings → General → VPN & Device Management).

## Development
Needs Node 24 and npm. Run `npm install` once, then:

- `npm run dev`: start the app on http://localhost:3000
- `npm run build`: production build
- `npm test`: unit tests (Vitest)
- `npm run test:e2e`: end-to-end tests (Playwright, desktop Chrome and iPhone WebKit); starts the dev server if none is running. `E2E_PORT=3001 npm run test:e2e` (PowerShell: `$env:E2E_PORT=3001; npm run test:e2e`) uses another port, for a second checkout.
- `npm run test:offline`: production build, then the offline check on http://localhost:3100 (Playwright, desktop Chrome)
- `npm run cert:https`, then `npm run start:https`: the production build over HTTPS for a phone on your Wi-Fi (see Try it on your iPhone)
- `npm run typecheck`: TypeScript check
- `npm run lint`: ESLint

Playwright needs its browsers once: `npx playwright install chromium webkit`. A failed Playwright test keeps a trace: `npx playwright show-trace test-results/<test>/trace.zip`. In CI, a failed run uploads the HTML report and the traces as the `playwright-report` artifact.

### Accounts and the database (optional)
Without Supabase settings the app runs local-only and hides the account UI. To try accounts:

1. Create a Supabase project in region Frankfurt. Under Authentication, turn on Email, turn **Confirm email** off, and set the minimum password length to 8.
2. Copy `.env.example` to `.env.local` and fill in the Project URL and the **Publishable** key (Project Settings → API Keys). Never put a secret key there. Restart `npm run dev` after a change: the values are built into the app.
3. Apply the migrations in `supabase/migrations`: `npx supabase login`, `npx supabase link --project-ref <ref>`, then `npx supabase db push`, or paste each file into the SQL editor.

With Docker running, `npm run supabase:start` starts a local Supabase stack with the migrations applied (Studio on http://127.0.0.1:54323), `npm run test:db` runs the database tests (pgTAP, in `supabase/tests`) against it, `npm run test:contract` checks the app's account store against it (after `node scripts/supabase-test-env.mjs` has printed the two settings it needs), and `npm run supabase:stop` stops it. CI runs them on every pull request.

### StudentsBudget in Claude (MCP server)
`mcp/` is a local [MCP](https://modelcontextprotocol.io) server. It lets Claude answer from the app's own Regensburg data and from your own budget, and it isn't part of the web app.

**What it does:**
- Five read-only tools over the Regensburg data: `regensburg_costs`, `find_discounts`, `find_tips`, `suggest_swaps` and `semester_info`.
  - Every figure comes with its source, URL and checked date, and the tools tell Claude to cite them. A fee no page states is never guessed.
  - They need no account and send nothing anywhere.
- `budget_overview` and `recent_entries`, also read-only, read your StudentsBudget account:
  - `budget_overview` runs the app's own maths on it: what is left this month and this week, each envelope, and the account balances.
  - `recent_entries` lists a month's entries, newest first, optionally for one category.
  - The data is as of your last **Save to my account** on the phone.
  - It signs in with two more lines in `.env.local`: `STUDENTSBUDGET_MCP_EMAIL` and `STUDENTSBUDGET_MCP_PASSWORD` (see `.env.example`). The password sits there in plain text; the file is git-ignored. Use a test account first.
  - Your figures and entry notes go into the Claude chat.
- Claude reads every answer into the chat.

**Claude Code:** open this repo, and Claude Code offers the `studentsbudget` server from `.mcp.json`. Approve it, or use `/mcp`. Then ask something like "What does a room cost in Regensburg?" or "Which student discounts are there for swimming?".

**Claude Desktop:** in Settings → Developer → Edit Config, add the server to `claude_desktop_config.json` with the full path to this checkout, then restart Claude Desktop:

```json
{
  "mcpServers": {
    "studentsbudget": { "command": "node", "args": ["C:\\path\\to\\StudentsBudget\\mcp\\server.ts"] }
  }
}
```

It needs Node 24 on the PATH and `npm install` done in the checkout.

**By hand:**
- `npm run mcp` starts the server on stdio. It waits for a client and logs to stderr.
- `npx @modelcontextprotocol/inspector@0.22.0 node mcp/server.ts` opens the official MCP Inspector (pinned, like everything else here), where you can call each tool.
- `npm test` includes the server's tests: a real MCP client over the SDK's in-memory transport.
